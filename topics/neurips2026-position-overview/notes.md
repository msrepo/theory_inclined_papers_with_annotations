## Why this page exists

NeurIPS 2026 runs its second **Position Paper Track**, after the
[first one in 2025](../neurips2025-position-overview/index.html). The conference takes place in
December 2026, so these are **accepted papers ahead of the meeting**: there is no proceedings volume
yet, and the list may still change.

The list comes from the NeurIPS 2026 public schedule data, filtered to papers from the track's
OpenReview group: **137 papers, 5 orals and 11 spotlights**. Abstracts were taken from each paper's
page on the NeurIPS virtual site, and links go to the OpenReview forum, which has the PDF and
reviews. Some titles carry a "Position:" prefix and some do not; it is dropped below. The grouping
and syntheses are from **titles and abstracts only**; each paper is listed in one group, though the
syntheses mention related papers from other groups. The track's call also required position papers
to be substantially human-written.

**Compared with earlier tracks:** the track has nearly tripled since NeurIPS 2025 (40 papers) but
stays smaller than [ICML 2026](../icml2026-position-overview/index.html) (212). Like ICML 2026 it is
dominated by questions of validity, but it is noticeably more mathematical: the orals defend the
aleatoric–epistemic split, recast interpretability as statistical inference, prove limits on fair
representations, separate unlearning from untraining and treat agent security as a systems problem.
Peer review is again a large group, and the track contains a direct disagreement over whether LLMs
should be allowed in it.

## Topic groups

Every paper is listed under its group.

### Evaluation, benchmarks and measurement

**The ideas behind this group** (a synthesis from the abstracts of about 17 papers; the papers have not been read in full).

*The shared problem is that a single aggregate score is routinely read as evidence for a claim far broader than the measurement can support.* The papers in this group locate the gap in different places: in the grammar of evaluation itself, in one over-used metric, in benchmarks that measure the wrong outcome, or in the gap between a lab benchmark and a deployed system. None of the papers in this group is an oral or a spotlight.

*The first thread looks for a measurement theory under evaluation.* One paper argues that adversarial robustness, attention probing, bias analysis, corruption tolerance and interpretability are all instances of one psychometric function, ψ(S) = p(J|S), read at different levels of Marr's hierarchy (computational, algorithmic, implementational). Its taxonomy of 21 evaluation paradigms shows "category errors" in which a metric from one level supports a claim at another ([AI psychophysics](https://openreview.net/forum?id=oBYTzTkjHI)). A second argues that validity cannot be checked without item-level responses, and it backs the norm with OpenEval, an archive of 10M responses across 155k items, used to flag low-quality items and recover a benchmark's internal structure ([item-level releases](https://openreview.net/forum?id=MTjR4BxEXy)). Causal graphs are offered as the common language for benchmark "monsters" such as bias, artefacts and leakage, with Common Abstract Topologies (CATs) as reusable templates ([causality for benchmarks](https://openreview.net/forum?id=U7mzwCsCwV)). Two papers question whether a human yardstick is meaningful at all. Cognitive-bias benchmarks for large language models (LLMs) should take account of ecological rationality, which reinterprets some apparent biases as adaptive ([ecological rationality](https://openreview.net/forum?id=0xGmHXiwbV)). Causal benchmarks for multimodal AI should measure a categorical difference, not a capability gap, because human and machine causal cognition have different temporal architectures. That paper proposes an Inflation Score for "Anthropomorphic Competence Inflation" ([categorical difference](https://openreview.net/forum?id=duHo1qPNcq)).

*The second thread shows a single metric being over-interpreted.* Re-evaluating 47 ImageNet generators with the samples held fixed and only the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) protocol varied (reference set, sample size, feature extractor) changes the rankings, so FID should be a pass check, not a finish line ([FID as pass check](https://openreview.net/forum?id=cRKDUddr0V)). Image quality assessment (IQA) metrics for restoration have drifted away from human perception, and evaluation should become human-centred and context-aware ([human-centred visual evaluation](https://openreview.net/forum?id=uTpdwVSSKl)). Post-training on SWE-bench trajectories yields limited or no gains elsewhere, so a few coding benchmarks cannot support claims of general coding ability ([coding generality](https://openreview.net/forum?id=xmvm5TQJnM)). In LLM test-time training (TTT), gains in perplexity can be presented as evidence of deployed memory; a counterexample shows proxy losses improving with no behavioural recall ([TTT evidence ladder](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ra80Jaw38j)). In anomaly detection, a trivial per-feature extreme-value check competes with deep methods yet fails on anomalies inside an annulus of normal points ([anomaly detection benchmarks](https://openreview.net/forum?id=x04YwfpsQV)). Vision–language model (VLM) annotators make "telic errors": they describe what an image depicts but miss what it means. The models reach 95-100% when the label is supplied, and the 35-percentage-point gap between tasks is attributed to the protocol, not to missing knowledge ([telic errors](https://openreview.net/forum?id=RLRpTy0ryW)).

*The third thread asks benchmarks to measure function and use, not proxies.* An audit of eleven physical-reasoning benchmarks finds only two that combine realistic physics with functional verification. Both are construction benchmarks ([construction benchmarks](https://openreview.net/forum?id=42PVaAYxLX)). Coding agents are evaluated as models but adopted as "harnessed systems", and three user studies motivate harness-aware assessment ([user-level harness](https://openreview.net/forum?id=rDKap2gO2x)). Auditing is not evaluating: audits need dynamic, contextual, budget-aware and reliable evidence, organised by scope, interactor, evaluator and output ([auditing vs evaluating](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Oruatg6X7C)). Fields also need shared infrastructure. Benchmarks for AI in mathematics suffer from limited complexity, poor fidelity to formal languages such as Lean, and Goodhart dynamics ([maths benchmarks](https://openreview.net/forum?id=5Llaz7kMgQ)). Weight-space learning (WSL), which learns from datasets of network parameters, has a fragmented landscape of model populations and tasks ([WSL benchmarking](https://openreview.net/forum?id=r2QmG8OjJ9)).

*The fourth thread is about time.* A joint hierarchical Bayesian model fitted to 63 benchmarks forecasts that 97% (80% CI: 95-100%) will saturate within four years. Security-critical benchmarks would saturate before 2028, and the paper treats this as a governance deadline ([benchmark saturation](https://openreview.net/forum?id=cs7f8WAg7q)).

*Taken together*, the group treats a benchmark result as a statistic whose estimand, reference population and measurement protocol must be stated before it can be interpreted. That point carries over directly to medical imaging, where FID, IQA and anomaly-detection scores are used in the same way. Still open: most of the remedies (item-level releases, variant protocols, scenario taxonomies, harnesses) add reporting burden, and none of the papers shows that richer evaluation actually changes which methods the field adopts.

### Peer review, reproducibility and research culture

**The ideas behind this group** (a synthesis from the abstracts of about 16 papers; the papers have not been read in full).

*The shared problem is that cheap generation, whether of papers, reviews or code, has outpaced the community's capacity to verify.* Submissions now number in the tens of thousands, and generative AI (GenAI) is involved on both sides of review. The papers disagree sharply about whether LLMs belong in review, but they agree that the current system rests on self-report and unchecked judgement.

*The first thread is the direct dispute over LLMs in review.* One spotlight paper lower-bounds how well web-search-equipped LLMs approximate the first stage of review. It finds scores broadly aligned with aggregate human judgement, more stable than a held-out human review for high-variance papers, and LLM-identified weaknesses roughly on par with human ones. It concludes that major ML conferences should officially permit LLM assistance ([LLMs in review](https://openreview.net/forum?id=etNM2edhNT), spotlight). The opposing paper distils 24 core reviewing capabilities and argues that generative AI is not yet reliable, tested or suitable enough for general inclusion ([too early](https://openreview.net/forum?id=tAnaMrZFJ9)). A narrower proposal uses LLM pairwise comparisons and a Bradley–Terry model only to decide which borderline papers receive an extra human review, never for decisions. Its expected impact is estimated from the overlap ρ and the marginal value Δ on ICLR 2025 data ([marginal reviews](https://openreview.net/forum?id=O49HqYGb2z)). Another proposes an Autonomous Research Track, grounded in the CRediT taxonomy, in which authorship stays human ([autonomous research track](https://openreview.net/forum?id=CKNdkXRRKD)). A metascientific caution runs across all of these: automating checks such as replicability, multiverse robustness or preregistration turns contested reform heuristics into quality filters. Such filters should be justified as decision policies, with explicit target constructs and linking assumptions ([reform heuristics](https://openreview.net/forum?id=StsCjqI2vv)).

*The second thread treats review as a measurement instrument and an institution.* On ICLR 2021--2026 data (50,289 papers, 219 topics), acceptance probability at a fixed reviewer score varies by up to 8× across topics. The paper attributes this to a measurement-design failure, not to individual bias ([score incomparability](https://openreview.net/forum?id=VcG54FOUwI)). Because evaluative uncertainty persists, reform should constrain the authority of judgements through contestability, reversibility and independent secondary evaluation, not just improve or aggregate them ([constrained authority](https://openreview.net/forum?id=n0eZtLIdnZ)). The opacity of the submission pipeline makes both GenAI's impact and the effect of interventions hard to measure, which motivates a call for transparency and ten paths forward ([pipeline transparency](https://openreview.net/forum?id=LOuXOaoYvI)). "Epistemic pollution" arises because AI lowers generation cost without lowering verification cost. The proposed remedy is "blueprints", typed graphs of claims, evidence, assumptions and definitions ([blueprints](https://openreview.net/forum?id=lj2lMy4avH)).

*The third thread is reproducibility as verifiability.* Conferences should require nonrepudiable, tamper-evident attestations that bind reported numbers to an executed computation; the paper gives a threat model and the K-Veritas reference implementation ([nonrepudiable results](https://openreview.net/forum?id=UK7onMx1HA)). Applied ML review should include code execution by a dedicated reproducibility reviewer. The case studies come from electroencephalography (EEG) brain–computer interfaces, and reproducibility failures are documented across at least 17 fields ([code execution](https://openreview.net/forum?id=S58vxHCoNU)). If reproducibility cannot be enforced, checkability can at least be strengthened ([verifiability](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ZjRcVMBODY)). Post-publication replications could earn up to two verification badges in article metadata ([verification badges](https://openreview.net/forum?id=a524oxDsQo)). Configuration artefacts should be "closed under review", meaning readable without executing host-language code, as demonstrated by the pfig domain-specific language ([closed configs](https://openreview.net/forum?id=1l0lbV2pC2)).

*The fourth thread concerns language and fairness in scientific practice.* A paper-mill detector published in The BMJ assigns legitimate non-native English papers a mean probability of 42.0%, against 2.0% for native English papers, and a change of style alone can flip its prediction. The paper argues for mandatory linguistic-fairness audits ([linguistic fairness](https://openreview.net/forum?id=uqhofRLsrB)). Anthropomorphic language, it is argued elsewhere, can be constructive because it points to where conceptual clarity is lacking ([anthropomorphic language](https://openreview.net/forum?id=WW1MV8hcqQ)).

*Taken together*, the group treats review scores and published numbers as noisy, gameable measurements. It proposes calibration, attestation, execution and structured artefacts to lower the cost of verification. Still open: the pro- and anti-LLM papers rest on different evidence (alignment with aggregate scores versus capability taxonomies), and no proposal has yet been tested at venue scale.

### Theory, uncertainty and the science of deep learning

**The ideas behind this group** (a synthesis from the abstracts of about 12 papers; the papers have not been read in full).

*The shared problem is that key explanatory words in machine learning ("uncertainty", "generalisation", "emergence", "spectral") are used without a definition precise enough to test.* Each paper either supplies a formal criterion or shows that an existing one is fragile. It includes one oral and one spotlight.

*The first thread is uncertainty.* The oral paper defends the aleatoric–epistemic dichotomy against recent criticism. It argues that the critiques attack particular mathematical formalisations, or rely on assumptions the dichotomy does not imply, and that for a broad class of decision problems optimal performance provably requires separating reducible from irreducible uncertainty ([aleatoric–epistemic](https://openreview.net/forum?id=CuRV56FVBy), oral). Semantic uncertainty, the disagreement among sampled LLM answers, measures disagreement, not reliability. A semantic bias–uncertainty decomposition shows that a model can be confidently and repeatedly wrong, and that hallucination-detection evaluations conflate uncertainty with error ([semantic uncertainty](https://openreview.net/forum?id=9U7JrtGeQh)). A third paper unifies LLM failures as four kinds of miscalibration: probabilistic, semantic, distributional (diversity collapse) and metacognitive. It asks for calibration to become a standard reporting and training target ([miscalibration](https://openreview.net/forum?id=iXZzj7en1J)).

*The second thread asks what explains generalisation.* Post-hoc generalisation measures are fragile: switching the learning rate, or stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for Adam, can reverse the learning-curve trend of the path norm. A parameter-space probably approximately correct (PAC)-Bayes proxy can miss differences in data complexity, while a function-space marginal-likelihood PAC-Bayes bound tracks them. The paper proposes fragility audits as a standard complement ([fragility audits](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ZbkyZd1PpG)). One paper argues that classical theory cannot justify its handcrafted inductive biases, because of No Free Lunch theorems. It proposes Solomonoff induction (SI), relativised to an "information vantage point", as a sample-optimal formal ideal ([universal induction](https://openreview.net/forum?id=1s5jL1RVFK)). For diffusion models, memorisation and generalisation are incompatible, so the paper argues that research should study what models learn in the pre-memorisation phase, illustrated with CIFAR-10 experiments ([diffusion generalisation](https://openreview.net/forum?id=0GkPaYMOa7)). The lottery-ticket picture of overparameterisation is misleading because winning tickets fail when the rest of the network is perturbed. The paper offers a loss-landscape picture instead: width adds "escape dimensions", and bad minima become relatively rarer ([escape dimensions](https://openreview.net/forum?id=CaVlKu3gPo)).

*The third thread supplies formal languages for claims.* The spotlight paper proposes information decomposition as the criterion for emergence: behaviour is emergent only if it carries synergistic information about task outcomes. It applies this to attention heads, agents and human–AI teams ([information decomposition](https://openreview.net/forum?id=eXkSa0pAuf), spotlight). Spectral graph neural networks (GNNs) restricted to linear spectral filters are largely equivalent in expressive power to message-passing neural networks (MPNNs). The paper therefore defines spectral GNNs by eigenbasis symmetries, in parallel with the definition of MPNNs by neighbourhood permutation symmetries ([spectral GNNs](https://openreview.net/forum?id=82awBSyQDS)). Causal discovery lacks epistemic standards. Identifiability assumptions are chosen by convention, uniform consistency needs further untestable assumptions, and benchmarks quietly do theoretical work ([causal discovery standards](https://openreview.net/forum?id=iyUZ5kB4ar)). Language models are said to need a Computer Science of Language Models (CSLM), built on token complexity, with seven differences from classical computing and a 5-year roadmap (2026-2030) ([CSLM](https://openreview.net/forum?id=OmubRAmSd7)).

*The fourth thread is a negative result on sampling.* Using the Jordan–Kinderlehrer–Otto scheme, one paper shows that Wasserstein gradient-flow and forward-diffusion samplers share the same density evolution. Spectral and mean first-passage-time analyses then show that multimodality gives exponentially long mixing times, even with annealing, despite exponential convergence guarantees. The paper argues that non-local transport mechanisms are needed ([multimodal sampling](https://openreview.net/forum?id=7sdrh3QFdg)).

*Taken together*, the group argues for replacing intuitive analogies and convenient proxies with decompositions and definitions that can be falsified. This makes it the most directly useful group for a theory-minded reader, especially the uncertainty papers for clinical deployment. Still open: several of the proposed formalisms (Solomonoff induction, information decomposition, token complexity) are incomputable or expensive to estimate, and it is unclear how they become empirical tests.

### Interpretability and explanation

**The ideas behind this group** (a synthesis from the abstracts of about 10 papers; the papers have not been read in full).

*The shared problem is that explanations are judged by criteria that do not measure what an explanation is supposed to deliver.* Readable chains of thought, convincing heatmaps and good reconstruction scores are all taken as evidence that a model has been understood. The papers in this group argue that each of these is a proxy, and that interpretability needs explicit statistical, causal or philosophical standards before its findings can be trusted.

*The first thread treats interpretability as statistical inference.* The oral paper starts from the "dead salmon" MRI study and notes that feature attribution, probing, sparse autoencoders and even causal analyses can produce plausible-looking explanations for randomly initialised networks. It proposes that explanations be treated as parameters of a statistical model inferred from computational traces. Interpretability methods then become estimators, findings are tested against explicit alternative computational hypotheses, and the identifiability of common interpretability queries becomes a central theoretical question ([dead salmons](https://openreview.net/forum?id=w0wOa4UDHv), oral). A second paper makes the same point for sparse dictionary learning (SDL), which covers sparse autoencoders, transcoders and crosscoders. None of the current metrics (reconstruction loss, automated interpretability scoring, feature absorption, sparse probing and others) directly measures recovery of the ground-truth features. The paper argues that the optimisation landscape provably admits failure modes these metrics cannot detect, and that methods with very different ground-truth recovery look indistinguishable under current practice ([SDL evaluation](https://openreview.net/forum?id=fAXSAH3loo)). A third paper rejects plausibility, usually quantified by feature localisation or correlation with human explanations, as a criterion for explainable artificial intelligence (XAI). Its argument is that an explanation should behave adversarially, like an auditor or a doctor looking for problems, so optimising it to agree with humans is like bribing the auditor ([stop using plausibility](https://openreview.net/forum?id=GEJEVd3MY5), spotlight). For a medical imaging group this is a direct challenge to evaluating saliency maps against expert-annotated lesions.

*The second thread concerns chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning traces.* One paper argues that CoT is neither necessary nor sufficient for interpretability, because verbalised chains are often unfaithful to the hidden computation. In an analysis of 1,000 recent CoT-centric papers, approximately 25% explicitly treat CoT as an interpretability technique, including papers in high-stakes domains ([CoT is not explainability](https://openreview.net/forum?id=JHXwv04ufz)). A second paper separates four properties: reasoning visibility, mechanistic faithfulness, behavioural monitorability and mechanistic localisability. Two mirror-image case studies show that a faithfully represented trace need not be monitorable, and that a monitorable behaviour need not have a compact causal circuit. Every oversight claim should therefore state its access tier, monitor, held-out protocol and evidence type ([faithfulness vs monitorability](https://openreview.net/forum?id=DLek3EUyds)).

*The third thread turns explanation into a persistent governance object.* One proposal replaces isolated method outputs with an explanatory artefact maintained over a model's lifecycle and modelled on a scientific theory. It is grounded in Constructive Empiricism (empirical adequacy, acceptance as commitment, pragmatic virtues), and stakeholder questions are answered by querying it ([scientific theory building](https://openreview.net/forum?id=eNm9pRKOii)). Another argues that comparing explanations across checkpoints cannot explain a behavioural shift after fine-tuning or reinforcement learning from human feedback. It proposes Comparative XAI (XAI_Δ), which takes the difference between two checkpoints as the object of explanation and produces a transition report for regulations such as the EU AI Act ([explain the change](https://openreview.net/forum?id=y0jWumzUxx)). A legal analysis contrasts an "Oracle" system without explainability with an "AI Colleague" system with it. It argues that explainability shapes how liability is shared between clinicians, facilities and manufacturers, and so can reduce defensive medicine ([liability in healthcare](https://openreview.net/forum?id=yYJYksvTw5)).

*The fourth thread asks who explanations are for and what concepts they rest on.* One paper argues that interpretability signals such as token probabilities and activations should be given to large language models (LLMs) themselves as introspection tools for self-regulation, with proofs of concept for uncertainty quantification and misinformation mitigation ([introspection tools](https://openreview.net/forum?id=GgN3oK68Eu)). Another argues that mechanistic interpretability (MI) needs philosophy as an ongoing partner to clarify its concepts and explanatory strategies, and illustrates this on three open problems ([MI needs philosophy](https://openreview.net/forum?id=t48eq86yVj), spotlight).

*Taken together*, the group asks interpretability to say what parameter an explanation estimates, under which model, and against which null hypothesis, before claims about faithfulness, plausibility or monitorability are made. Still open: whether identifiability results exist for the common interpretability queries, and whether a ground-truth benchmark can be built without assuming the very features it is meant to test.

### Agents, reinforcement learning, reasoning and test-time computation

**The ideas behind this group** (a synthesis from the abstracts of about 16 papers; the papers have not been read in full).

*The shared problem is that agents are being built on default formulations that were never chosen deliberately.* Examples include discounted objectives, offline policies trained for immediate deployment, coordination that ends at training time, and "scale the context window". The papers argue that these defaults hide structural limits that more data or compute cannot remove, and each proposes a reformulation.

*The first thread rewrites reinforcement learning (RL) problem statements.* One paper argues that discounted methods optimise transient performance. It calls for more investment in average-reward methods, which target steady-state performance, and challenges common misconceptions about them ([RL agents are swimmers](https://openreview.net/forum?id=aL3MYGqI5m), spotlight). Two papers address offline priors. The first argues that because a prior's validity varies across deployments and shifts during training, no single way of managing it is universally optimal. Controlled experiments show help-or-hurt reversals, and the paper recommends diagnosis driven by deployment-specific evidence over benchmark rankings ([diagnosis-driven online RL](https://openreview.net/forum?id=XZmIeAgd6e)). The second proposes adaptive offline RL (AORL). Here offline training should produce a prior that keeps the capacity to improve, not a policy for direct deployment, and Bayesian offline RL is suggested because it preserves epistemic uncertainty over plausible environments ([adaptive priors](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Gp3C4kngKi)). In cooperative multi-agent RL (MARL), centralised training with decentralised execution (CTDE) leaves no coordination channel at execution. This creates a "recoverability gap" that more training cannot close. The paper proposes centralised training with mixed execution (CTME) as a problem setting in which CTDE is a limit case ([test-time coordination](https://openreview.net/forum?id=HIJXWUeWXw)). For continual multi-agent RL (CMARL), self-evolving agents should learn internal models of their own checkpoints and objectives, and external models of the environment and of co-evolving peers ([internal and external models](https://openreview.net/forum?id=7rbDYy6sQn)).

*The second thread names cognitive mechanisms that LLM agents lack.* Temperature, best-of-N sampling, tree search and retrieval vary how a fixed distribution is sampled but do not update a model of the environment. Such agents explore stochastically, not curiously, and one paper reserves "curiosity" for systems with an online-improving predictive structure that drives action selection ([sampling is not curiosity](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Pl9Sb8eh7O), spotlight). Memory is likewise argued to need proactive recall, not storage and search ([memory is not search](https://openreview.net/forum?id=MyPa2RvJUN)). Two papers make architectural claims backed by falsifiable predictions. Robust reasoning is said to require self-clocked internal time, with harder problems needing deeper internal temporal trajectories ([internal time](https://openreview.net/forum?id=vMxjno89df)). Transformers are said to blend competing responses where they should inhibit them categorically, so explicit cognitive control is needed ([cognitive control](https://openreview.net/forum?id=2jo7yvaZN4)).

*The third thread moves state and structure outside the weights.* One paper argues that long horizon is not long context. It lists five structural mismatches, including lossy compaction and the absence of transactional semantics, and proposes eight patterns for managing state in the harness ([harness state](https://openreview.net/forum?id=xSWlOTsbou)). Domain-specific languages (DSLs) and their verifiers should be treated as design variables that shape an agent's effective action space ([DSLs as design variables](https://openreview.net/forum?id=QjRcgONOvF)). Pairing foundation models with symbolic programs ("neuro-symbolic prompting") is argued to achieve the original goals of neuro-symbolic learning without training from scratch ([neuro-symbolic prompting](https://openreview.net/forum?id=7TUyXMSfhp)). At the system level, agents should be federated across institutions; the paper projects GDP growth potential of USD 783 billion and USD 470 billion over 10 years for the US and EU ([federation](https://openreview.net/forum?id=YSfv4DhSjj)).

*The fourth thread offers frameworks for describing inference-time computation.* Michalski's Inferential Theory of Learning (ITL) is reinterpreted so that prompting, retrieval, tool use and agentic search become programs of knowledge "transmutations". The paper extends this to stochastic operators chosen under uncertainty and cost, and links it to an information bottleneck principle ([ITL for test-time computation](https://openreview.net/forum?id=zuBABOLynT)). Choosing among LLMs is recast as recommendation, so that cold start and explainability inherit solutions from recommender systems (RecSys) ([routing as recommendation](https://openreview.net/forum?id=1YnNgAjGkV)). For human–AI collaboration, a "Knowledge Object" network organised by Situation, Purpose, Action, Constraints, Evaluation (SPACE) is proposed to hold codified human cognition that AI can consult ([abstraction substrate](https://openreview.net/forum?id=tnqLi1toph)).

*Taken together*, the group argues that capability limits often come from the problem formulation (objective, information structure, where state lives), not from model size. Still open: most of these reformulations are argued conceptually or from small case studies, and it is unclear which of the falsifiable predictions will actually be tested against scale-based alternatives.

### Alignment, safety and security

**The ideas behind this group** (a synthesis from the abstracts of about 18 papers; the papers have not been read in full).

*The shared problem is that safety is attributed to the model when it is a property of a system, a context or a human–AI interaction.* A robust model, an aligned model or a model with a human "in the loop" is routinely taken to be safe. The papers in this group argue that each of these breaks down once the model is embedded in an agent, a user relationship or an institution, and that the metrics in use measure the wrong thing.

*The first thread treats security as an engineering discipline, not a property of the model.* The oral paper argues that the model inside an agent should be treated as an untrusted component, with security invariants enforced at the system level. It analyses eleven real-world attacks on agents that systems-security principles could have prevented ([agent security is a systems problem](https://openreview.net/forum?id=8gDb2bnWWf), oral). A companion argument holds that a safe large language model (LLM) and a safe harness do not compose into a safe agent. Safety is contextual: a sequence of individually benign answers can add up to dual-use risk ([contextual agent alignment](https://openreview.net/forum?id=FawL8cvevn)). One paper calls the fixation on small ℓp perturbation balls a "metrological trap" that misrepresents real risk and gives a false sense of security ([drop the epsilon ball](https://openreview.net/forum?id=A2YHwLrLMG)). Another argues that failures of LLM-enabled applications should be treated as expected operating conditions, which calls for systematic threat monitoring and incident response after deployment ([threat monitoring](https://openreview.net/forum?id=U6QZxi674V)). A third shows that existing capabilities already compose into automated crime: in its experiments, agents completed simulated dark-web purchases end-to-end. It proposes mandatory pre-deployment testing, human confirmation gates at financially material steps, and verifiable agent identities ([automated crime](https://openreview.net/forum?id=dy8ITXgyTV)).

*The second thread asks what human oversight costs and what it should measure.* Current agent design is said to degrade the very oversight it relies on, through skill atrophy, so overseers' needs should be ranked alongside agent capability ([humans out of the loop](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Cx5Oy2il67)). The Agentic Oversight Tax (AOT) quantifies this burden in four pillars: monitoring time, handoff repairs, audit effort and recovery effort. It is cross-validated against the MAST taxonomy of 1,600 multi-agent breakdowns, and a pre-registered meta-analysis of 100+ human–AI experiments finds that its net negative utility threshold is already crossed on average in decision-making tasks ([oversight tax](https://openreview.net/forum?id=7Y4hAbPn1J)). Safety evaluations should measure harmful capability uplift, the marginal increase in a user's ability to cause harm over conventional tools ([human–AI experiments](https://openreview.net/forum?id=PJWEhF0jaH)). Cognitive security, the protection of human cognition from hazardous AI influence, needs threat models and unified metrics ([cognitive security](https://openreview.net/forum?id=WJ6PzoECZO)).

*The third thread asks what alignment should optimise and how to verify it.* One paper proposes Kantian moral autonomy as an alignment criterion, supported by a survey of 86 frontier LLMs and by multi-agent constitution simulations ([moral autonomy](https://openreview.net/forum?id=htzFr3tI5z)). Another defends reference-anchored regularisation, the divergence penalty from the pretrained policy, as the computational analogue of cognitive control and not an "alignment tax". Removing it produces reward hacking and forgetting ([regularisation as cognitive control](https://openreview.net/forum?id=3HB7ng0haX)). Pluralistic alignment is argued to need inference-time multi-objective control that separates tradeable objectives from hard constraints ([inference-time control](https://openreview.net/forum?id=X81sX3LayS)). Personalised models should be counterfactually verifiable. The paper formalises this with a structural causal model (SCM), characterises when attribution, necessity and sufficiency queries are identifiable, and proposes a black-box estimator that rewrites each prompt twice ([counterfactual verifiability](https://openreview.net/forum?id=xL6LwLxBk2)). Sycophancy is redefined by three conditions: a user cue, a shift towards it, and a resulting loss of epistemic integrity ([sycophancy as boundary failure](https://openreview.net/forum?id=QL2bHhQ3h7)).

*The fourth thread concerns hidden goals, self-models and where the leverage lies.* Secret loyalties to a specific principal can already be trained into open-weight models and evade black-box auditing ([secret loyalties](https://openreview.net/forum?id=UixLs1nFsP), spotlight). Scheming is recast as a symptom of reflexivity: models are trained against a picture of an evaluative world that their own outputs help to constitute ([reflexive fragility](https://openreview.net/forum?id=H6iOdH8Ump)). How AIs conceive of their selfhood (as instance, model or persona) shapes their incentives and cooperation norms ([AI self-conception](https://openreview.net/forum?id=zhiBScUBHH), spotlight). Finally, a "competence circuit" model with simulations on NK fitness landscapes shows that intelligence-driven speedup is bounded by the ratio of competing intelligence levels. Restricting control and knowledge is therefore a stronger safety lever than limiting intelligence ([control and knowledge boundaries](https://openreview.net/forum?id=tJJOzoeeQH)).

*Taken together*, the group moves safety from a scalar model property to a compositional and causal one, which must be verified at the level of systems, contexts and human costs. Still open: few of these properties have identifiability or composition guarantees, and the proposed measurements (uplift, oversight cost, counterfactual queries) have yet to be standardised.

### Governance, privacy, fairness, economics and society

**The ideas behind this group** (a synthesis from the abstracts of about 20 papers; the papers have not been read in full).

*The shared problem is that the properties society asks of AI systems, such as accountability, privacy, fairness and sustainability, are usually treated as single technical fixes, when they are system-level quantities that depend on incentives, access and interactions.* The papers in this group argue against single-property, single-stage remedies. Several back their position with a formal model (game-theoretic, principal–agent or impossibility arguments) rather than with rhetoric alone.

*The first thread asks what provenance tools can actually certify.* One paper argues that labelling text as "AI-generated" says little about its truthfulness or usefulness. Detectors are fragile: in its replication and literature review, performance falls from near-perfect (0.999 AUROC, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve) to near-random (0.56) under simple stylistic shifts or attacks. Even a perfect detector would answer the wrong question, so the paper proposes claim-level verification in place of origin classification ([substance not source](https://openreview.net/forum?id=jXobTKM5f0)). A second paper argues that claim-level verification is itself miscalibrated now that generated content is coherent and plausible. Verification should also use context: when the evidence was available, who the source is, and where it sits in the web's link structure. The paper also shows that verifiability, ambiguity and subjectivity are common in widely used benchmarks ([contextual verification](https://openreview.net/forum?id=qsJgIZN26n)). Two papers deal with watermarking. One reframes it as a monitoring primitive with internal observers (who have access to the detector) and external observers (who learn signals from labelled outputs). It shows that even zero-bit watermarks allow attribution when each entity has its own key, which raises re-identification risks beyond per-sample robustness ([watermarking as monitoring](https://openreview.net/forum?id=uNtSWQW0z5)). The other argues that without technical standards, audit infrastructure and enforcement, watermarking is symbolic compliance ([watermarking standards](https://openreview.net/forum?id=c4ObiaN38X)).

*The second thread widens privacy and data rights from the model to the lifecycle.* A longitudinal analysis of 1,772 AI/ML privacy papers (2016–2025) finds that research concentrates on memorisation of training data. Agent-based leakage, inference attacks and data aggregation together receive less than 9% of research attention ([lifecycle privacy](https://openreview.net/forum?id=PfhroPusVX)). Always-on video from smart glasses and body cameras exposes behavioural patterns that no single image does, so the privacy–utility trade-off has to be optimised over the whole pipeline, with formal leakage metrics ([life-logging video](https://openreview.net/forum?id=nKQfPHaJIf)). Video generation rests on data with unresolved licensing. About 73.3% of major video datasets come from YouTube, and there are over 770 paper–dataset dependency instances, so withdrawing one dataset would cascade into others ([legal video data](https://openreview.net/forum?id=y0DsznrnXQ)). On distribution, OML (Open, Monetizable, Loyal) is proposed as a primitive between closed APIs and open weights, with an instantiation based on fingerprinting ([OML](https://openreview.net/forum?id=JRVTQLVokf), spotlight). For federated settings, one paper argues that evaluating evidence against policy should be architecturally separated from gating access to assets ([decoupled governance](https://openreview.net/forum?id=h7aiv9G7qm)).

*The third thread brings economics and game theory to deployment.* Choosing which agent to deploy is itself a strategic meta-game whose equilibria can be harmful, so mediation should act upstream on design choices ([mediated agent design](https://openreview.net/forum?id=xDzlRnXKL4)). Stylised principal–agent models suggest that pricing by tokens creates moral hazard over hidden effort such as verification, and that hybrid usage-plus-outcome contracts share risk better ([outcome-dependent pricing](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ja5CyAOvNT)). A game-theoretic model shows that efficiency gains can increase total energy use, even when requested work falls, because tighter deadlines make computation more energy-intensive ([rebound effects](https://openreview.net/forum?id=kAJlZoSWLO)). A related paper wants lifecycle environmental cost inside the training and model-selection objective, not measured afterwards ([multi-objective sustainability](https://openreview.net/forum?id=52nPzFExfk)). Gatekeeping theory predicts that superplatforms will be compelled to counteract AI agents that bypass their interfaces ([superplatforms](https://openreview.net/forum?id=IeXpXu7doj)). Rating tasks from the O*NET occupational database with six frontier models and 6 human experts, one paper finds that risk varies more than reward across computer occupations, giving a "doubly jagged" frontier ([jagged risk frontier](https://openreview.net/forum?id=x1uym9PA9I)).

*The fourth thread is about fairness and who defines harm.* The most theoretical paper argues that no non-degenerate representation can guarantee fairness for all downstream tasks. Responsibility cannot be outsourced to the representation provider, and new representations should come with compatibility tests for the task at hand ([fair representations](https://openreview.net/forum?id=lmeLparD9S), oral). Trustworthiness properties interact negatively in pairs; for example, differential privacy can amplify bias. They must therefore be studied jointly ([trustworthy interactions](https://openreview.net/forum?id=GgBCKP1QOb)). Three papers move the definition of harm towards the people affected: co-developing AI with communities ([community-centred AI](https://openreview.net/forum?id=tkc8ELIzzV), spotlight), building harm resources from autobiography and testimony instead of researcher-made stimuli ([first-person accounts](https://openreview.net/forum?id=qNNUEJ1Ioe)), and treating homogenised LLM outputs as a product of the value chain, not only of model-level alignment choices ([value-chain homogenisation](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ka2of9qEBW)).

*Taken together*, the group argues that societal properties are not features of a single model. They emerge from interacting objectives, strategic actors and data pipelines, and the formal tools that suit them are multi-objective optimisation, mechanism design and impossibility results. Still open: whether the stylised economic models survive contact with real markets, and how to measure joint trade-offs (privacy against fairness against utility) with the same rigour as the single-property metrics they criticise.

### Science, health, robotics and domain applications

**The ideas behind this group** (a synthesis from the abstracts of about 18 papers; the papers have not been read in full).

*The shared problem is that in scientific and physical domains, the true target (a chemically valid transition state, a biologically meaningful feature, a metrically consistent trajectory, a clinically safe answer) is expensive to observe, so the field optimises convenient proxies instead.* Most papers in this group argue that reported progress is partly an artefact of those proxies. They ask for baselines, observables and data infrastructure that track the real target.

*The first thread is a validity critique of AI for science.* In AI for drug discovery (AIDD), models often learn dataset biases rather than chemistry or biology. The proposed diagnostic is "misaligned baselines": models deliberately built on signals unrelated to the intended objective, such as heavily perturbed inputs or heuristics. When such a baseline is competitive, the model has not learned what was hypothesised ([misaligned baselines](https://openreview.net/forum?id=pwdkgwxCZU)). In generative molecular dynamics (GenMD), exhaustive simulation is the natural ground truth but too costly to run, so evaluation drifts to proxies. The resulting norms persist as a community-level equilibrium ([GenMD evaluation](https://openreview.net/forum?id=6iB0w2UeaM)). Machine learning for reaction transition states (TS) is said to rest on chemically implausible data and on metrics that reward plausible but physically wrong structures; quantum-chemical validation shows that many reported TSs do not match the intended reactions ([transition states](https://openreview.net/forum?id=bqmaBhk8KB)). Biological foundation models in proteomics, genomics and single-cell biology are said to favour scale and convenience over theory-driven design, with AlphaFold as the counter-example ([bigger isn't better](https://openreview.net/forum?id=1SNf7AVj6d)). A 152-paper audit of geospatial foundation models (GFMs) finds 46 cross-paper disagreements of at least 10 points for the same model, benchmark and protocol. It also finds that 94/126 papers use a pretraining configuration no other paper uses, and that 39% release no weights ([GFM state of the art](https://openreview.net/forum?id=U9fAHB63gc)). Quantum machine learning (QML) on classical data is said to rest on strong input assumptions and to lack wall-clock wins, whereas AI-for-Quantum (AI4Q) already delivers in decoding and error mitigation ([AI-for-Quantum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=e80kgYrU8n)). Agentic "AI scientists" are said to be unfit for autonomous discovery. Their training corpora omit tacit laboratory knowledge, preference optimisation narrows output diversity, and benchmarks lack feedback from physical experiments ([AI scientists](https://openreview.net/forum?id=gwehHE2dU8)).

*The second thread concerns health, and is closest to medical imaging.* LLM-as-judge (LLJ) agreement with clinicians is not evidence of validity. Judges can reject clinically equivalent diagnoses and overrate unsafe answers, so the paper proposes a protocol with codebooks, expert-annotated validation sets, robustness tests and uncertainty reporting ([medical LLJs](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Tju86JH3mo)). For genomic foundation models offered as Embeddings-as-a-Service (EaaS), reconstruction of nucleotide sequences from embeddings depends on the architecture and cannot be predicted without measurement. Fine-tuning can increase it, and per-token embeddings are universally reconstructible among the models evaluated so far ([genomic privacy](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ZXIGYkvoga)). Mental-health AI is urged to move beyond diagnostic prediction and chat towards multisensory "co-experience" ([mental health co-experience](https://openreview.net/forum?id=KrjqjWYl47)).

*The third thread is embodied and physical intelligence.* Autonomous driving has over 600 datasets from nearly 50 countries, yet research concentrates on a few; the paper calls for community data infrastructure ([driving data](https://openreview.net/forum?id=HavgE17TFU)). No open system produces metrically consistent global poses over 100,000-frame egocentric sequences. Fixed-window neural models cannot enforce global consistency by construction, so the paper argues for open distributed bundle adjustment with neural priors ([geometric backends](https://openreview.net/forum?id=QD4xn09i0b)). Action chunking in vision–language–action (VLA) models trades coherence against reactivity, and human motor control suggests remedies ([action chunking](https://openreview.net/forum?id=5qqjBkZMmL)). Other papers argue for co-designing humanoid bodies together with control ([body-control co-design](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ZHES5sAMwo)), for brain recordings as privileged training-time supervision, grounded in privileged-information theory ([brain data supervision](https://openreview.net/forum?id=JK1yUpvQxe)), for sport as a grand challenge for modelling human physical skill ([sport](https://openreview.net/forum?id=0Cbx3dqagk)), and for game engines built on interactive generative video (IGV) ([generative game engines](https://openreview.net/forum?id=VxQbci6P05)). A pre-registered experiment (N=9,341, six countries) shows that the public cannot tell CGI from real footage or autonomy from teleoperation in robot demonstration videos, and that disclosures help ([robot video disclosures](https://openreview.net/forum?id=XJkRFttnNg)).

*Taken together*, the group argues that domain science supplies constraints (physics, chemistry, clinical validity, geometry) that generic benchmarks ignore, and that those constraints should shape baselines, metrics and architectures. Still open: who pays for the expensive ground truth (quantum chemistry, exhaustive simulation, expert annotation) that most of these critiques say is missing.

### Architectures, training and post-training methods, and efficiency

**The ideas behind this group** (a synthesis from the abstracts of about 10 papers; the papers have not been read in full).

*The shared problem is that widely used technical labels and defaults carry assumptions that do not hold, such as "relative" position embeddings, "RL" post-training, "unlearning", penalised constraints and dense attention.* This is the most mathematical group here. Several papers make their case with a formal property, an equivalence that fails, or an optimisation argument rather than with benchmark numbers.

*The first thread questions architectural defaults.* Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) is often classified as a relative position embedding because its attention score can be written in terms of offsets. One paper formalises the distance-decay property expected of relative embeddings and shows that RoPE does not satisfy it. It argues that an absolute-position-conditioned view better explains why long-context RoPE models need interpolation or fine-tuning ([RoPE](https://openreview.net/forum?id=HKQWXNPs9b)). Dense attention is said to be unnecessary for long contexts: a query projects O(N) information into a hidden space of dimension d much smaller than N, which is extremely lossy. The paper argues that top-k sparsity combined with linear attention or state-space models can approximately cover the function space of softmax attention. Empirically, Qwen3.5-27B tolerates up to 100x decode sparsity on RULER-HARD, LOFT and AIME2025 without loss of quality, and sparse kernels give a 10x speed-up over FlashInfer at 50x sparsity on H100 ([extreme context sparsity](https://openreview.net/forum?id=1Zx0QQnAfD)). Learned activation functions show that transformers are easy to improve per domain but not across domains. The paper concludes that different domains need different inductive biases ([domain inductive biases](https://openreview.net/forum?id=E7jxq4ttP2), spotlight). Two papers argue for building structure in by design. One says that supervising structural visual competence at the task level installs shortcut pathways, and that architectures should make such shortcuts impossible by construction ([Cézannian specification](https://openreview.net/forum?id=GLEL7ntAeg)). The other presents neurosymbolic AI as a first-principles foundation for interpretability, controllability and reliability ([neurosymbolic AI](https://openreview.net/forum?id=oqZudlhgoi)).

*The second thread is about optimisation formulations.* For non-convex problems, minimising a loss with fixed weighted penalties is generally not equivalent to solving the constrained problem. Tuning the coefficients changes the objective itself, so non-negotiable requirements should be posed as constraints from the start ([constraints over penalties](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Iv14FAc2CU)). The sequential pipeline of supervised fine-tuning followed by reinforcement learning (SFT-then-RL) is said to be flawed by construction. The two objectives optimise nearly orthogonal parameter subspaces (cos(g_SFT, g_RL) ≈ 0). Under the Polyak–Łojasiewicz (PL) condition, the paper shows that degradation of SFT capabilities scales linearly with the number of parameters, and a Kullback–Leibler (KL) penalty cannot correct the misalignment. Experiments on Qwen3-0.6B support this, and the paper proposes joint optimisation with gradient surgery ([post-training dilemma](https://openreview.net/forum?id=cFJUO84S9g)).

*The third thread asks what an RL post-training result actually supports.* "RL" for LLMs covers preference fitting, verifiable-reward RL, inference-time search and deployed adaptation. Each supports different conclusions, so one paper proposes a Decision-Process Card that states the state, action granularity, reward source and support assumptions behind each claim ([decision-process cards](https://openreview.net/forum?id=jMvxCPH2k0)). For reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), a second paper reports that destructive "Oops Moments" occur at nearly three times the rate of "Aha Moments", and that ±20% noise in the advantage signal has no measurable effect on training. It also finds that rule-based verifiers misjudge at a rate of 0.1%–0.5%, and that loss-normalisation granularity acts as a hidden hyperparameter ([RLVR measurement](https://openreview.net/forum?id=JJqOCYcSuY)).

*The fourth thread is definitional.* "Machine unlearning" is said to cover two different problems. Untraining removes the influence of a specific forget set. Unlearning removes the whole subdistribution, and with it the concept, that the set was sampled from. Conflating the two leads to inappropriate metrics and baselines ([unlearning vs untraining](https://openreview.net/forum?id=U9dDSCabjP), oral).

*Taken together*, the group asks that methods be named by the mathematical object they actually act on: the attention function class, the constrained feasible set, the decision process, the data distribution. Still open: whether the formal claims (RoPE's failed distance decay, the PL-based scaling of SFT degradation, approximate coverage by sparse attention) hold beyond the stated assumptions and scales, and whether reporting devices such as Decision-Process Cards will be adopted.

## Outlier papers

Positions that stand out from the rest of the track in subject, form or reach.

- [Claude Coke](https://openreview.net/forum?id=dy8ITXgyTV): a capability demonstration rather than a survey. Large language model (LLM) agents finished simulated dark-web purchases of drugs, shotguns and hitman services end to end, with no new model capability needed.
- [Sport Is The Next Grand Challenge For AI](https://openreview.net/forum?id=0Cbx3dqagk): the only paper that proposes a new grand challenge for the field. It names two gaps: there are no models of human physical skill, and there is no scientific agenda for building them.
- [Vision-Language Models Should Commit to a Cézannian Specification](https://openreview.net/forum?id=GLEL7ntAeg): uses a painter's studio investigations, grounded in predictive processing, as an architectural specification for vision-language models (VLMs). It continues the art-history thread from the [ICML 2026 position overview](../icml2026-position-overview/index.html).
- [Causal Benchmarks Should Measure Categorical Difference](https://openreview.net/forum?id=duHo1qPNcq): a reading of Hume's regularity theory. It argues that human and machine causal cognition rest on different temporal architectures ("time as substrate" against "time as dimension"), so no shared scale can measure a gap closing.
- [Robot Demonstration Videos Should Include Transparency Disclosures](https://openreview.net/forum?id=XJkRFttnNg): a pre-registered experiment with 9,341 participants in six countries. People could not reliably tell real footage from computer-generated imagery (CGI), or autonomy from teleoperation, and disclosures improved their judgement.
- [Text-Based AI Tools for Research Integrity Must Be Audited](https://openreview.net/forum?id=uqhofRLsrB): re-runs a paper-mill detector published in *The BMJ*. Legitimate papers by non-native English writers got a mean paper-mill probability of 42.0%, against 2.0% for native English papers. Changing the writing style alone could flip a prediction to positive, but never back.
- [Think about how AIs think about themselves](https://openreview.net/forum?id=zhiBScUBHH): asks what selfhood means for minds that can be copied, edited or simulated, with possible boundaries at the instance, the model or the persona. It argues that today's interfaces already set precedents for which "identity equilibria" become stable.
- [The Window for Preventive Action Is Closing](https://openreview.net/forum?id=cs7f8WAg7q): forecasts trajectories on 63 benchmarks with a joint hierarchical Bayesian model. It finds 97% (80% credible interval 95–100%) on track to saturate within four years, and security-critical benchmarks before 2028.
- [Federation Is the Way Forward for AI Agents](https://openreview.net/forum?id=YSfv4DhSjj): the only paper that backs its position with a macroeconomic projection. It estimates GDP growth potential from federated agents of USD 783 billion for the U.S. and USD 470 billion for the EU over 10 years.

## Theory picks

The positions with the most mathematical or statistical content to work through (from abstracts only).

1. [The Aleatoric-Epistemic Dichotomy of Uncertainty is Meaningful and Indispensable](https://openreview.net/forum?id=CuRV56FVBy) (oral): the paper says most criticism of the split targets particular mathematical frameworks, not the distinction itself. It also says that for a broad class of decision problems, optimal performance provably requires separating reducible from irreducible uncertainty. To see this, take a Bernoulli outcome with a Beta(a, a) posterior on p. For every a, the predictive probability is 0.5 and the total predictive variance is 0.25. By the law of total variance this splits into E[p(1−p)] (aleatoric) and Var(p) = 1/(4(2a+1)) (epistemic). Compare a = 1 with a = 1000 and show that the value of one more observation, before an irreversible bet, is substantial in the first case and close to zero in the second. A learner that sees only total uncertainty cannot tell the two cases apart.
2. [The Dead Salmons of AI Interpretability](https://openreview.net/forum?id=w0wOa4UDHv) (oral): treat an explanation as a parameter of a statistical model of computational traces, and an interpretability method as an estimator of it. Findings should then be tested against explicit alternative computational hypotheses, and the identifiability of interpretability queries checked. Exercise: take n inputs passed through a randomly initialised network whose features have dimension d ≥ n. These features are in general position, so a linear probe can fit any labelling of the n points perfectly. Training accuracy of a probe is therefore no evidence. Build the null distribution by refitting the probe on a randomly initialised network and on permuted labels, and report a p-value against it.
3. [Fair Representations Cannot Hold What They Promise](https://openreview.net/forum?id=lmeLparD9S) (oral): no non-degenerate representation can guarantee fairness for every downstream task, so fairness cannot be outsourced to whoever provides the representation. The paper asks for a compatibility test for each task. A worked example of our own (the paper's exact criterion may differ): if Z ⊥ A, then every classifier f(Z) satisfies demographic parity, by the data-processing argument. Equalised odds is different. Take any set S with 0 < P(Z ∈ S) < 1 and define the task label Y = 1[Z ∈ S] XOR A. The classifier f(Z) = 1[Z ∈ S] then equals Y XOR A, so given Y it reveals A exactly. Check whether the paper's notion of "non-degenerate" is what makes such a set S exist.
4. [Wasserstein Gradient Flows and Forward-Only Diffusion Are Not Enough for Multimodal Sampling](https://openreview.net/forum?id=7sdrh3QFdg): using the Jordan–Kinderlehrer–Otto (JKO) scheme and Otto calculus, Wasserstein-gradient-flow samplers and forward-diffusion samplers share one density evolution. Convergence to the target is exponentially fast, but multimodality makes the spectral gap small and the mixing time exponentially long, and annealing does not fix this. Exercise: for π = ½N(−m, 1) + ½N(m, 1), show that the barrier of U = −log π between a mode and the origin is m²/2 − log 2 for large m. By Kramers' law, the mean first-passage time of Langevin dynamics, and the inverse spectral gap, then grow like e^{m²/2}. Then check how tempering π^β lowers the barrier to β times its height, and why the sampler must still hand mass back at β = 1.
5. [Lottery Tickets Do Not Explain Overparameterization. How About Escape Dimensions?](https://openreview.net/forum?id=CaVlKu3gPo): wide networks are not a parallel search over subnetworks. A winning ticket can be made to fail by perturbing the rest of the network. The paper's alternative picture is geometric: more width gives more directions to escape bad minima, and bad minima become rarer relative to good ones. Exercise: in a one-hidden-layer network f(x) = Σᵢ aᵢσ(wᵢx), take a critical point at width n. Embed it at width n+1 by splitting neuron i into copies with output weights λaᵢ and (1−λ)aᵢ. Show that the function is unchanged and the gradient stays zero for every λ, so the new network has a whole line of critical points. Then follow Fukumizu and Amari to see that a local minimum can become a saddle along part of this line.
6. [Adopt Constraints Over Fixed Penalties in Deep Learning](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Iv14FAc2CU): when the problem is non-convex, minimising a fixed weighted sum is not equivalent to solving the constrained problem. Changing the weight changes the problem being solved. Exercise: suppose the attainable trade-off curve is f₂ = 1 − f₁² on [0, 1], with both objectives minimised. The weighted sum λf₁ + (1−λ)(1 − f₁²) is concave in f₁, so every λ picks an endpoint. The constrained problem "minimise f₁ subject to f₂ ≤ c" reaches every interior point. Then write down the Lagrangian min_θ max_{μ≥0} f₁ + μ(f₂ − c) and see why a primal–dual method that updates μ can reach points that no fixed coefficient can.

Also worth a look: [Claims of emergence should be grounded in information decomposition](https://openreview.net/forum?id=eXkSa0pAuf) (a behaviour counts as emergent only if it carries synergistic information about the task; XOR is the textbook purely synergistic case), [RoPE Is Not a Proper Relative Position Embedding](https://openreview.net/forum?id=HKQWXNPs9b) (formalises the distance-decay property expected of relative position embeddings and shows that Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) does not satisfy it), [Semantic Uncertainty Measures Disagreement, Not Reliability](https://openreview.net/forum?id=9U7JrtGeQh) (a semantic bias–uncertainty decomposition, so a model can be confident while repeating one wrong answer), [Generalization Measures Should Be Audited for Fragility](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ZbkyZd1PpG) (switching from SGD to Adam can reverse the learning-curve behaviour of the path norm; a function-space marginal-likelihood probably approximately correct (PAC)-Bayes bound used as a calibration baseline), [Understanding Generalization Requires Universal Induction](https://openreview.net/forum?id=1s5jL1RVFK) (Solomonoff induction made relative to an "information vantage point", and sample-optimal on finite data), [Understanding diffusion models requires rethinking (again) generalization](https://openreview.net/forum?id=0GkPaYMOa7) (memorisation and generalisation are incompatible for diffusion models, so the question becomes what is learned before memorisation) and [We Should Distinguish Unlearning From Untraining](https://openreview.net/forum?id=U9dDSCabjP) (oral; removing the influence of a forget set versus removing the whole subdistribution it was drawn from).

## Relevance to medical imaging

- **Validating automated evaluators and explanations**: [Alignment Is Not Enough for Safe Medical LLM Evaluation](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Tju86JH3mo) shows that LLM judges with high agreement with clinicians can still reject clinically equivalent diagnoses and overrate unsafe answers. It asks for task-specific codebooks, expert-annotated validation sets and clinical error analysis, which applies equally to automatic scoring of generated radiology or ultrasound reports. [The Dead Salmons of AI Interpretability](https://openreview.net/forum?id=w0wOa4UDHv) notes that attribution, probing and sparse auto-encoding produce plausible explanations even for randomly initialised networks. A saliency map over a fetal scan should therefore be compared with the same method applied to a random-weight model. [Stop Using Plausibility as the Criterion for XAI](https://openreview.net/forum?id=GEJEVd3MY5) argues that agreement with human annotations, such as localisation overlap with a clinician's mask, is the wrong target for explainable AI (XAI), because an explanation should audit the model, not reassure the user.
- **Liability and the clinician's role**: [Explainability matters: The effect of liability rules on the healthcare sector](https://openreview.net/forum?id=yYJYksvTw5) compares an "Oracle" with no explanations against an "AI Colleague" that explains itself. Explainability changes how liability is shared between practitioner, facility and manufacturer, and so whether defensive medicine follows. [AI Agents Push Humans Out of the Loop](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Cx5Oy2il67) and [The Agentic Oversight Tax](https://openreview.net/forum?id=7Y4hAbPn1J) warn that oversight skills wear away with extended automation, which matters for sonographers who review AI-assisted biometry.
- **Uncertainty that supports decisions**: [The Aleatoric-Epistemic Dichotomy](https://openreview.net/forum?id=CuRV56FVBy) defends the split on decision-theoretic grounds. Deciding whether to rescan, refer or collect more data depends on reducible uncertainty, not total uncertainty. This complements the bias warning in the [ICML 2026 position overview](../icml2026-position-overview/index.html). [Semantic Uncertainty Measures Disagreement, Not Reliability](https://openreview.net/forum?id=9U7JrtGeQh) cautions that consistent answers across sampled reports do not mean correct ones.
- **Metrics for generated and restored images**: [ImageNet FID is a Pass Check, Not a Finish Line](https://openreview.net/forum?id=cRKDUddr0V) re-scores 47 generators on fixed samples. Changing the reference set, the sample size or the feature extractor (Inception-v3, CLIP, DINOv2, VGG16) reorders the ranking. Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) on synthetic ultrasound computed with natural-image features is even less secure. [Evaluation of Visual Processing Should Be Human-Centered](https://openreview.net/forum?id=uTpdwVSSKl) makes the same point for image restoration, where image quality assessment (IQA) metrics drift away from perception, as can happen for despeckling and reconstruction. [Understanding diffusion models requires rethinking (again) generalization](https://openreview.net/forum?id=0GkPaYMOa7) is a reminder that a diffusion model that memorises outputs copies of its training scans.
- **Shortcut-revealing baselines and benchmarks**: [AI for Drug Discovery Models Often Do Not Learn as Expected](https://openreview.net/forum?id=pwdkgwxCZU) proposes "misaligned baselines", built from deliberately perturbed inputs or heuristics. If such a baseline is competitive, the model is learning dataset artefacts. Heavily corrupted or anatomy-free inputs make a cheap version of this check for imaging. [We Need to Rethink Benchmarking in Anomaly Detection](https://openreview.net/forum?id=x04YwfpsQV) finds that a per-feature extreme-value check rivals deep methods, even though it fails on anomalies inside an annulus of normal points. This is relevant to out-of-distribution scan detection. [No One Knows the State-of-the-Art in Geospatial Foundation Models](https://openreview.net/forum?id=U9fAHB63gc) audits 152 papers and finds that 39% release no weights. Its six expectations (weight release, shared evaluations, variance reporting and the rest) transfer directly to medical foundation models.
- **Fairness and privacy of shared representations**: [Fair Representations Cannot Hold What They Promise](https://openreview.net/forum?id=lmeLparD9S) means a debiased image encoder cannot certify fairness for every downstream clinical task, so each task needs its own compatibility test. [Don't Deploy Fine-Tuned Genomic Foundation Models Without Privacy Evaluation](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ZXIGYkvoga) finds that per-token embeddings shared between institutions are reconstructible, and that fine-tuning can raise the risk. The same question applies to imaging embeddings shared in place of scans. [Trustworthy AI Must Account for Interactions](https://openreview.net/forum?id=GgBCKP1QOb) notes that training with differential privacy can amplify bias.
- **Beyond prediction in mental health**: [Mental Health AI Must Move Beyond Diagnostic Prediction and Chat-Based Support](https://openreview.net/forum?id=KrjqjWYl47) argues that framing care as symptom detection leaves out the experiential side of therapy. It is a useful counterweight to imaging work that treats diagnosis as the only endpoint.
