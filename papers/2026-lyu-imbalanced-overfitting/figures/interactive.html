1. Training logits vs test logits: the truncation

The data are the paper's two-Gaussian mixture: y = +1 (minority) with probability π, and x | y ~ N(yµ, Id), with n/d → δ. Train the max-margin classifier f(x) = ⟨x, β⟩ + β₀ with ‖β‖ = 1. Theorem 2.1 says that on a test point the signed logit y·f(x) is N(ρ*‖µ‖ + yβ₀*, 1). On the training points it is the same Gaussian, except that everything below the margin κ* is moved to a point mass at κ*. The shaded area below 0 is the test error, and training erases it. The numbers come from solving Eqs 76a–c.

Things to try. At the default (Figure 1 of the paper), 61% of minority training points sit exactly on the margin, against 18% of majority points. The minority's per-point push g₁(t₊) is always exactly (1−π)/π times the majority's. The reason is Eq 79: each class's total push is equal, and the minority has fewer points to share it. Drag δ up towards δ*(0) and κ* shrinks to 0. Drag it down and the truncation gets more severe. τ leaves ρ* alone and slides β₀* (Corollary E.10). Press τ = τopt and the two test errors become equal. Now set π = 0.05 and δ = 0.5. τopt turns negative: no positive τ reaches β₀* = 0. Press train an SVM to overlay a real hard-margin SVM (dual SMO, d = n/δ) on the limit.

2. When is the training set separable? δ*(0)

Theorem D.1(a): with probability tending to 1 the data are linearly separable if δ < δ*(0), and not separable if δ > δ*(0). Here δ*(0) = max over ρ, β₀ of (1−ρ²) / E[(−ρ‖µ‖ − G − β₀Y)₊²]. The page finds it as the δ at which κ* crosses 0. With ‖µ‖ = 0 and π = ½ this is Cover's classical n/d = 2. The intercept, the signal and the imbalance all raise it.

The dot is widget 1's current (π, δ).

The rebalanced SVM (Eq 7) asks for a margin τ times larger on the minority side. Proposition C.1 says this changes only the intercept: β₀(τ) = β₀(1) + (τ−1)/(τ+1)·κ(1). 4. High imbalance: three phases

Theorem 3.2 lets things grow with d: π ∝ d−a, ‖µ‖² ∝ db, n ∝ dc+1, and τ = dr. The whole story is a competition between two lengths. One is the signal ‖µ‖ ≍ db/2. The other is the noise that the minority's few training points can be fitted to: the norm of their mean noise, √(d/n₊) ≍ d(a−c)/2. Lemma H.1 bounds the margin by half the distance between the two training class means, ≈ √(db + da−c/4).

Left: the (a − c, b) plane. Above the line a − c = b the signal wins outright and any 1 ≤ τ ≪ db/2 works. Below a − c = 2b the signal is lost and no τ helps. In between, the moderate wedge, the plain SVM (r = 0) gets the minority wrong almost always. Only τ in the window da−b−c ≪ τ ≪ d(a−c)/2 fixes it. Right: that window on the r axis. The numbers in the readout are order-of-magnitude estimates from Lemmas H.1–H.5 at the chosen d. They are not exact limits.

5. Non-separable data: the proximal shrinkage

When δ > δ*(0) the SVM does not exist, so the paper studies logistic regression instead (Theorem D.3). There a typical training logit is proxλ*ℓ(test logit), where proxλℓ(x) = argmint ℓ(t) + (t − x)²/(2λ), and ℓ(t) = log(1 + e−t). A small λ* (large δ, little overfitting) gives nearly the identity. A large λ* (δ just above δ*(0)) gives a smooth version of the truncation max{κ, x}, up to scale. The paper does not give λ* in closed form (it solves a 4-equation system), so here λ is a free slider.

6. Calibration: the limiting reliability curve

With ‖β‖ = 1 the confidence is p̂ = σ(f). By Bayes' rule on the two Gaussian logit classes (Lemma I.1), the truth given that logit is p̂₀ = σ(2ρ*‖µ‖·(f − β₀*) + log π/(1−π)). So the limiting reliability diagram is one explicit curve, the paper's Figure 6 without the binning. There are two separate mismatches. The slope 2ρ*‖µ‖ need not equal 1, which is a scale that ‖β‖ = 1 fixes arbitrarily. And at τopt (β₀* = 0) the classifier ignores the prior log-odds. The toggle adds the prior back, to show how much of CalErr* each one accounts for.
